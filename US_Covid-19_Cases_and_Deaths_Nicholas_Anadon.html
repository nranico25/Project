Instructor Credit and Data Introduction: Dr. Jane Wall was a sensational instructor for “Data Science as a Field” and per her permission on the final project, she has granted permission for the clean-up code of “COVID-19 Data Repository by the Center for Systems Science and Engineering (CSSE) at Johns Hopkins University” on Github.com. This project gives a cleaned version of COVID-19 cases and deaths strictly within the United States. The time period of these cases and deaths reported starts at January 23, 2020 to March 9, 2023. The goal is to analyze what months had more cases and deaths than others and what are the summary statistics behind the months with the most daily differences in COVID cases and deaths.

Data set URL: https://github.com/pomkos/john-hopkins-covid-data

#Values of US cases
US_cases <- US_cases %>%

Timeframe of Covid-19 Cases and Deaths: The time frame graphs do not give a cumulative sum, but instead read the difference of new cases and deaths on one particular day compared to the day before. The drops in the graph represent zero or small negative change. Note the difference in scale between cases and deaths on the y-axis of the graphs where difference in cases reach over 1,000,000 on one day compared to deaths where the maximum amount of difference of deaths between two days was over 4000. The red line in the “New Deaths in US by Month” graph illustrates a slow growth of deaths per million of people.

New Cases and New Deaths Analysis:

New Cases

The day that had the greatest difference in new cases was +1,354,508 more cases on January 10, 2022 than January 9, 2022. Late fall 2021 - early winter 2022 was a time of extreme COVID-19 spread. On average, the difference in new cases was about +90,896. This average is heavily influenced by outliers of days with huge amounts of case differences especially during December, 2021 and Janurary, 2022. Even when the vaccine was distributed in mid-2021, cases started to explode toward the end of 2021 as people in America were trying to return to their standard way of life.

New Deaths

According to the new deaths boxplots, we can estimate the future death difference between two days is +985 deaths. This number does not make sense at the beginning of the pandemic, but as the virus continued to spread, the amount of deaths would steadily increase. The day with the greatest difference in deaths was January 12, 2021 with +4375 more people that died from COVID-19 compared to January 11, 2021. January, Feburary, and December have the greatest variability of daily death differences as the colder weather can lead to lower sunlight and weakened immune systems. There are many outliers in September and October due to a pattern similar to Influenza where as the weather starts to get colder and darker, cases and deaths steadily go up leading into winters.

Linear Model: If there is no difference in new cases presented on a day from the day before, we could estimate a death toll difference of about +648-649 people to die that same day. According to the linear estimates, for every difference of +270 new cases adds +1 difference of deaths. The adjusted R-squared value of 0.2531 indicates a weak, positive correlation between the daily difference of cases and daily difference of deaths, but not enough to indicate a very strong correlation. This certainly would explain the extreme spread of the data especially during seasonal changes.

Project Bias: There are many instances of bias that we need to account for:

-Different states have different COVID protocols. We can not assume that the method of measurements were the same throughout all states. Healthcare is different across all states as well.

-This dataset presents a huge emphasis on urban trends and areas. While rural areas are included, it is easy for these areas to be underrepresented.

-Considering that symptoms for some individuals registered as asymptomatic or mild, it would be wrong to assume that there were no more cases outside of those reported in this study.

Project Conclusion: January was the month with the greatest daily differences in COVID cases and the cold months of January, February, and December produce enormous variability of daily death differences compared to warmer months. While the relationship in differences between COVID-19 cases and deaths was not correlated to a powerful extent, the seasonal patterns align with extreme outliers as averages increase with colder weather. While a difference in +200-300 cases does not necessarily predict how many people will die, it is still an important statistic for public health information to take into consideration when examining other lurking variables. While we certainly should not leave out the warmer months from early spring to earlier fall, we can safely say that winter/colder months, especially January 2021 and January 2022, had the greatest COVID-19 outbreaks. In the future, if more pandemics are to occur, we can at least try to focus our efforts toward stronger healthcare in colder months and give individuals incentives that allow them to practice habits and activities to enhance their overall health.
